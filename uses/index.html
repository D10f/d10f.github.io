    • What I Use

    This is what I use every day to get things done.

    As I like to switch things around from time to time, I'm listing here various technologies that I've used and that I particularly like, or that I have installed in separate machines.

    Feel free to checkout my dotfiles to see how I configure these tools!

    Hardware

    Tuxedo Book XP14 Gen12

    A pretty decent general purpose computer that allows me to get through the day without much fuzz. The 14" screen makes it compact and lighweight to take on the go, and the keys are silent but pleasant to type with.

    Newskill Pyros Red 60% Mechanical Keyboard

    I have limited space, and so having a compact keyboard is essential to me. This was one of the cheapest I could find that would still live up to the task of long programming sessions every day. And it has not disappointed me.

    Logitech Ergo M575 Trackball

    This saves me a ton of space and neck pain from awkardly waving my arm across the desk every few minutes. Easily one of the best purchases in my entire setup.

    JVC HA-L50 Headphones

    There's nothing much to say about these headphones, which is why they are fantastic.

    Operating System

    Debian

    One of the oldest GNU/Linux distributions, well-known for its stability and reliability. I use this in both servers and desktop computers.

    Desktop

    XFCE

    A simple and lightweight desktop environment. It embodides the traditional UNIX philosophy of modularity, making it very simple to use and customize.

    i3

    A tiling window manager designed to be simple. It has plenty of options, however, and it integrates nicely with XFCE.

    dwm

    Another tiling window manager that excels at being extremely minimal. It's quite fun to play around with, but more importantly it's very lightweight, which is ideal for low-end devices.

    Terminal & Shell

    Alacritty

    My favorite terminal emulator. It's fast, and easy to configure using plain text files.

    St

    Another piece of software from the Suckless project that delivers on the promise of extreme minimalism.

    Bash

    The most popular shell used by default in most Unix systems. And for good reason: it's rock-solid, stable and reliable.

    Text Editor & IDE

    Neovim

    An excellent terminal-based text editor for simple text processing, as well as a full blown IDE for larger projects.

    VSCodium

    Free and open source version of Microsoft's VSCode. Works exactly the same but has all the telemetry and tracking removed.

    PyCharm

    JetBrains products are great for having all the goodies with zero configuration out of the box. In addition to PyCharm, I also reach out for Intellij for Java, RubyMine for Ruby, etc.

    Fonts & Themes

    Catppuccin

    My go-to color scheme when I'm not sure what to choose. Particularly useful for dark mode on poorly designed websites.

    Monokai Pro

    I prefer Catppuccin for most things, but I still use this one for VS Codium as I really like the color balance and icon design.

    And yes, I did buy the license!

    FiraCode

    A nice, readable font that I unfortunately rarely use outside of text editing.

    Web Browsing

    Mozilla Firefox

    Firefox remains one of the few browsers with plenty of configuration options. One of my favorite features is the ability to inject custom CSS to the interface itself, as well as overwrite websites' stylesheets.

    LibreWolf

    A privacy-focused fork of Firefox. It comes out of the box configured to protect your privacy.

    Vivaldi

    My go-to Chromium-based browser these days. It's highly customizable, though not as strong in terms of privacy.

    uBlock Origin

    A must-have extension to block ads, trackers and all sorts of unwanted junk.

    Vimium

    Once Vim's keybindings have taken over your muscle memory, it's impossible to go back! This extension helps navigating webpages using Vim bindings.

    SingleFile

    For archiving purposes, it's very useful to have an extension that downloads websites as single files.
